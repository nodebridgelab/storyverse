Monsoon at Platform Nine

by Ananya Rao · Romance · 3 chapters · Ongoing

Two commuters, one delayed local train, and a shared umbrella that neither of them can afford to return.

Chapter 1: The Shared Umbrella

The 6:42 to Dadar was cancelled, which was how Naina learned that the man with the blue umbrella had a laugh like a dropped steel plate: loud, clumsy and impossible to dislike.

'You can stand under it,' he said. 'It is a big umbrella and I am a small person.'

She was not small and he was not either, and the umbrella was not big. They stood with their shoulders almost touching while the rain made Mumbai look like a watercolour somebody had left on a windowsill.

Chapter 2: Same Time, Platform Nine

She told herself it was coincidence that she left home five minutes earlier on Wednesday. She told herself the same thing on Thursday.

His name was Dev. He taught mathematics to children who did not want it, and he carried a pocket notebook where he wrote problems he could not solve. On Friday he handed her a page. It said: If two people keep missing the same train on purpose, how many Fridays until one of them says so?

Naina took the pen from his hand and wrote underneath: Today.

Chapter 3: The Return of the Umbrella

The umbrella was never returned. It sat in her hallway, dripping onto the floor, a blue accusation.

In August, the monsoon broke early and the trains stopped for six hours. They waited at a tea stall, with strangers, with bad chai, with the whole city holding its breath.

'Keep it,' Dev said, folding the umbrella into her hands. 'I think I'd rather keep walking you to the station.'

She laughed, a little too loudly, and realised with some alarm that she had started to sound like him.
