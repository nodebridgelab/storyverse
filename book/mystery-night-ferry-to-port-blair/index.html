Night Ferry to Port Blair

by STORYVERSE Originals · Mystery & Adventure · 3 chapters · Completed

On a three-day sea voyage from Chennai, a botanist's rare orchid vanishes from a locked cabin. Young Kavya, travelling with her grandfather, notices the one thing every grown-up has overlooked.

Chapter 1: The Orchid in Cabin 41

The ship left Chennai harbour at dusk, and by the time the lights of the city had shrunk to a thread on the horizon, Kavya Ramanathan had already counted every lifeboat on the upper deck.

She was eleven and travelling with her grandfather, Appa Thatha, a retired surveyor who was going to Port Blair to see his old friend's grandchildren. It was a three-day voyage across the Bay of Bengal, and Kavya had a notebook for it, in which she meant to record everything.

The first thing worth recording was a scream.

It came on the second morning, from a cabin on the lower corridor. A woman in a green cardigan was standing in front of Cabin 41 with the steward and the purser, and she was speaking very fast. "It was in my cabin, locked in, and now it is gone!"

The woman was Dr. Rekha Prabhu, a botanist taking a rare orchid to a research garden in the islands. It was a small pale thing, a cluster of cream blossoms with purple throats, found only on one hillside in the Western Ghats, and she had carried it from Chennai in a glass dome padded with moss. She had gone to breakfast for twenty minutes. When she returned, the dome was gone.

"Locked from outside, madam," the steward insisted, jingling his keys. "And locked it was when you came back."

Kavya, who had been trailing the steward since breakfast, wrote in her notebook: The door was locked. The orchid is gone. Therefore the thief had a key.

Then she wrote a second line: Or the orchid was never in that cabin at all.

Chapter 2: Three Suspects and a Screw

The purser, Mr. Anwar, questioned everyone on the corridor. A retired sailor named Captain Rao occupied Cabin 14 at the other end, a gruff old man with a white beard. A young couple had Cabin 38, bickering softly through the walls. A salesman called Mr. Dutta had Cabin 40, next to the botanist, and he swore he had spent the morning on deck eating pakoras.

Appa Thatha sat Kavya down on the deck bench. "A detective does not guess," he said. "She looks at what is odd. Tell me, what is odd?"

"The door," said Kavya at once. "The steward says it was locked both times. So nobody got in with force. But the orchid left."

"Good. Now where do orchids go when nobody steals them?"

Kavya chewed her pencil. "Somewhere somebody thought they belonged."

She spent the afternoon walking the lower corridor, up and down, reading the brass number plates screwed to every door. 38. 40. 41. 42. They were neat, ordinary, and a little salty from the sea air. She stopped at the one marked 41, pulled a tiny torch from her pocket, and looked closely at the screws.

The top screw was loose. The plate wobbled when she touched it. And when she turned it, very slightly, the numeral 4 and the numeral 1 swung round on their single screw, so that a plate reading 41 could, with a light push, read 14.

Her heart began to thump. She ran the length of the ship, past the dining room and the radio cabin, to the far end of the corridor, to Cabin 14.

Chapter 3: The Gift for Cabin 14

Captain Rao opened the door on the second knock, in his vest and a very surprised expression. Behind him, on the little writing desk, stood a glass dome lined with moss, and inside it a cluster of cream blossoms with purple throats.

"Ah," he said, seeing Kavya's face, and then, much more slowly, "Ah."

By the time the purser and the botanist arrived, he was sitting on his bunk with the dome in his lap, looking sheepish and very old. "My niece Sumathi," he said. "She told me she would send me a surprise at sea, for my seventieth birthday. She said it would be waiting in my cabin, with a card. This was waiting in my cabin." He pointed to a small folded note on the desk. It said, in careful handwriting, For Cabin 14, with love.

"The steward put it there?" asked Dr. Prabhu faintly.

"No, madam," said Kavya. "I think the steward put it in the botanist's cabin, because the number plate on 41 had swung upside down. Someone read it as 14 and unlocked the door with the spare key. The note was for Captain Rao, and it was left on the botanist's desk. A small mistake, twice."

The purser stared at her. Then he started to laugh.

"The sailor's niece sent flowers," he said. "A real surprise, for a real captain. And the doctor's orchid, identical in the dark, sat in the wrong cabin for three hours."

"They are not the same flower," said Dr. Prabhu quietly, kneeling to inspect the blossoms. "This is my orchid. Rao-sahib, I am so sorry for your gift."

"Madam, I had to hide my gift in the cabin next door," said the old man gravely, "so I could not even look at it properly. Take it. I will find my niece a thank-you card instead."

Kavya wrote the solution in her notebook, in capital letters, and underlined it twice. A very tidy mystery, she decided, with a ferry's worth of sea around it and a loose brass screw at its heart.
