The Dog Walker

by STORYVERSE Originals · Romance · 3 chapters · Completed

Every morning they swap news about each other's dogs and carefully nothing else, until the dogs conspire.

Chapter 1: Dogs First

The park was a small one near the Malleswaram market — more of a garden, really, with a gravel path running its perimeter and a patch of grass in the middle that the municipality maintained with inconsistent enthusiasm. Devika came every morning at seven with Mango, a four-year-old Indie who had opinions about squirrels. Suresh came every morning at seven-fifteen with Biscuit, a three-year-old golden retriever who had opinions about everything.

Mango and Biscuit had, over the course of three months, developed a complex relationship that involved a great deal of circling each other, an occasional combined sprint around the park's perimeter, and what could only be described as a standing appointment — if one arrived first, it sat near the gate in a way that suggested waiting, which both owners chose not to comment on.

Devika and Suresh had arrived, also over three months, at an easy routine. They walked the perimeter in the same direction at about the same pace, and they talked about the dogs. This was not a decision they had made; it was simply what had happened. They knew a great deal about each other's dogs.

She knew that Biscuit had been anxious as a puppy and that Suresh had spent three months doing desensitization exercises with him. She knew Biscuit's vet, his preferred brand of treats, his irrational fear of the municipality truck that came on Wednesdays. She knew Biscuit had started to sleep in Suresh's bed, which had been a boundary that had not survived the first three weeks.

He knew that Mango had been found near the market and that Devika had intended to find her a home and then found that she herself was the home. He knew Mango's particular trick of sitting very close to someone without touching them, which Devika said was how Mango said she liked you. He had noticed, about a month ago, that Mango did this to him now, and he hadn't mentioned it.

Of each other they knew: first names, that the other also worked somewhere and came to the park before work, that they both drank coffee rather than tea based on the cups they occasionally carried. That was all. It had been enough, for three months, to be exactly what it was.

Chapter 2: Complications

In the fourth month, Biscuit ate something he shouldn't have near the park's east corner and Devika was the one who spotted it and said, calmly, 'He just ate a piece of plastic wrapping.' Suresh looked at Biscuit and then at her and said, 'How did you—' and she said, 'He does that. I've been watching for it since the first week. You should tell your vet.'

He looked at her with an expression that she couldn't quite categorize. He said, 'You've been watching my dog for four months.'

'We share a park,' she said.

'Right,' he said. He texted his vet. Biscuit was fine. But afterward there was a quality to the silence that had not been there before — not uncomfortable, but different. Aware.

She found herself thinking about his hands, which she had noticed because of the way he held Biscuit's leash — loosely, which said something about trust. She found herself making sure to arrive at seven rather than seven-ten, which she examined and then chose not to examine further.

He started arriving at seven, which she noticed.

Mango started going to sit next to him first, before coming back to her. Devika noticed this with a feeling she refused to name.

In the fifth month, it rained one Saturday and she didn't come to the park — she walked Mango around the block instead. On Monday, Suresh said, without preamble, 'Biscuit looked for Mango on Saturday.' He said it to the path in front of him. 'He waited at the gate for a bit.'

'It was raining,' she said.

'I know. I'm just telling you.' He paused. 'Biscuit noticed.'

'Just Biscuit,' she said.

'He's very observant,' Suresh said.

They walked the rest of the perimeter in a silence that was doing quite a lot of work.

Chapter 3: Off-Leash

The sixth month brought a Sunday when the park was nearly empty — a public holiday of some kind, the municipality workers home, the usual morning crowd absent. Mango and Biscuit ran together with the frantic joy of dogs who understood that unusual freedom demanded to be fully used.

Devika and Suresh sat on the bench near the middle patch of grass and watched their dogs. This was new — they had not sat before, only walked. Sitting was different. It felt slightly like declaring something.

'What do you do?' Suresh asked. Just like that, after six months.

She looked at him. 'Are we doing this now?'

'We've been talking about Mango and Biscuit for six months.'

'I know,' she said. 'I've been — I don't know why we didn't.'

'I think we both know why,' he said. He said it to the park rather than to her, which she appreciated. A way of stating the truth at a slight angle.

She was quiet for a moment. Mango crashed into Biscuit and they rolled and recovered and ran again. 'Graphic designer,' she said. 'Freelance. I work from home, which is why I can always come at seven.'

'Software,' he said. 'I work hybrid. Tuesdays and Thursdays in office, otherwise home.'

'Which is why you're always at seven-fifteen on Tuesdays and Thursdays,' she said. 'You're running slightly later those days.'

He turned to look at her fully. 'You know that.'

'Six months,' she said.

The dogs came back and collapsed near the bench in the companionable heap they had developed — Mango against Biscuit's side, Biscuit's chin on Mango's back — and lay there breathing.

'I'd like to know more than the dog facts,' Suresh said. He looked at the dogs, then at her. 'If that's—'

'There's a place near the market,' she said. 'Good filter coffee. They let dogs in, they have a water bowl at the door.' She looked at him. 'On weekends it's quiet until nine.'

'I know the place,' he said.

'Mango likes it,' she said.

'Biscuit too,' he said.

They sat there in the Sunday morning light until the dogs stood up and indicated, with the calm authority of animals who managed their people, that it was time to go somewhere. They went.
