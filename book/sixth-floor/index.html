The Sixth Floor Gamble

by Karan Malhotra · Business · 3 chapters · Ongoing

A bootstrapped founder has ninety days of cash, one impossible client, and a board that wants a miracle by quarter-end.

Chapter 1: Ninety Days of Cash

Aarav Khanna had two numbers memorised: 14 and 90. Fourteen employees. Ninety days of cash.

His start-up, Ledgerly, rented half a floor above a dentist in Gurugram. On quiet nights you could hear the drill through the carpet, and Aarav had begun to feel that the building itself was trying to tell him something about root canals and runways.

The email arrived at 9:17 p.m. A national bank wanted a demo. Friday. Their head of procurement had one condition: no slides. Only the product, live, in front of a room that wanted it to fail.

Chapter 2: The Room That Wanted It To Fail

The room had twelve people and zero smiles.

Aarav opened the laptop and did something he had never done in a pitch: he showed them a bug. A real one, in production, that had cost a customer forty thousand rupees the month before. Then he showed them how Ledgerly caught it in eleven seconds.

'You are the first vendor,' said the procurement head, 'who has shown me his mistakes before my own.'

Outside, in the lift, Aarav's hands began to shake. Trust, he thought, is just a bug you admit to before someone else finds it.

Chapter 3: Term Sheet

The offer came on a Tuesday: a term sheet, three pages, two investors, one clause that would take forty percent of everything he had built.

His co-founder, Meher, read it twice and slid it back. 'We can survive without them,' she said. 'The question is whether we want to survive or win.'

Aarav looked at the whiteboard, where fourteen names were written in Meher's neat hand. Every person was a rent payment, a family, a quiet bet on him. He picked up the pen. Some decisions, he realised, are not made with spreadsheets. They are made with names.
