The Tuesday Tiffin Club

by Zoya Ansari · Casual · 3 chapters · Ongoing

Five strangers on the fourth floor of a Pune software office swap lunch boxes every Tuesday. By the third week they are swapping secrets too.

Chapter 1: The Rule of Swapping

The rule was Rishi's, and he had invented it purely to escape his mother's lauki.

Every Tuesday, anyone with a tiffin could drop it into the middle of the pantry table, and anyone else could take a different one. No complaints, no returns, no asking who cooked what. By 1 p.m. the table looked like a small, delicious casino.

Priya from accounts drew a tiffin that smelled of coconut and curry leaves and nearly cried at her desk. Kabir from security drew her tuna sandwich and said, with great dignity, that he had never been so confused or so grateful.

Chapter 2: The Mystery Dabba

In week three, a tiffin appeared that nobody owned. Steel, three layers, tied with a faded green ribbon.

Top layer: jeera rice. Middle: dal so good it made two interns stop arguing about Kubernetes. Bottom layer: a note in careful handwriting that read, You all look like you need feeding. Sincerely, the cleaner on Floor 3.

The pantry went quiet. Then Kabir said what everyone was thinking: nobody in the building had ever learned her name. By Tuesday next, there were six new tiffins on the table and one chair pulled up at the end, with a place set for Sulochana-ji.

Chapter 3: A Seat at the End

Sulochana-ji sat down at 1:03 p.m. with her hands folded as if the chair might bite.

'I only cook extra,' she said. 'My husband used to eat four rotis. Now I still make four.'

Nobody laughed, and nobody pitied her either, which Rishi later said was the real miracle. Priya passed her the pickle. Kabir asked for the dal recipe and wrote it on his palm because he had no paper.

The club never got a name. But from then on, the pantry table had seven chairs, and the cleaner of Floor 3 was never again the last to be thanked.
