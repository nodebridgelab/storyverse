The Last Lighthouse of Kanyakumari

by Arjun Mehta · Mystery & Adventure · 3 chapters · Completed

A marine archaeologist returns to a decommissioned lighthouse to learn why three ships vanished in its final year of operation.

Chapter 1: The Iron Door

The monsoon had spent itself by the time Meera reached the tip of the subcontinent. Kanyakumari wore the aftermath well: wet granite shining like old coins, fishermen mending nets the colour of rust, and the sea on three sides arguing with itself about which direction to leave in.

The lighthouse stood where it had always stood, white as a fresh page. Its iron door was the same iron door from her grandfather's stories, with a wheel handle frozen mid-turn and a smell, even from ten paces away, of salt and cold lamp oil.

She had the keys. That was the strange part. Her mother had posted them with a note that said only: If you must go, go properly.

Chapter 2: Logbook, 1994

The logbook was on the third shelf of the oil room, wrapped in a fisherman's oilskin as if it could still get wet.

Meera sat on the bottom stair with her phone torch between her teeth and read her grandfather's handwriting for the first time in her life. It was small, slanted and infuriatingly calm. March 14th: Lamp trimmed. Visibility poor. The entry for March 15th was crossed out so hard the pen had torn the page.

It was only when she lowered the phone that she noticed the second staircase, the one that went down into the rock, where no lighthouse plan she had ever seen said a staircase should be.

Chapter 3: The Lamp Room

Two hundred and twelve steps. She counted them the way her grandfather must have counted them, four thousand times a year, one honest step at a time.

The lamp room was smaller than she had imagined and brighter, the great lens throwing the morning sun back in fractured petals across the walls. In the centre, where the lamp should have been, stood a wooden crate stamped with the mark of a company that had dissolved the year she was born.

Whatever her grandfather had kept in the light, he had kept it in plain sight. The best place to hide a secret, Meera thought, is where everyone expects the light to be.
